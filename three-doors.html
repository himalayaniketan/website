Know Mataji

Three Doors to One Truth

Mataji can be known in three ways: as the Person she was born, as the Grace others recognised in her, and as the Truth she points every seeker toward. Each is a door, and all three open into the same room.

The Human The Divine The Eternal

The First Door

The Human

She has always been Mataji.

Mataji - Maa Someshwari Devi was born as Monika Parsheera, the eldest daughter of a Rajput family from Lahaul, in the Pir Panjal range of the Himalayas. Hers was a regular household, with a grandfather who was a retired school teacher. Her days belonged to orchards, streams and a tree house, and her sister remembers her as wild and untamed.

Hardship was an early companion. Funds were scarce, and through her teenage years she was educated at a government school in Lahaul. At the age of 17, with winter closing the mountains, she crossed the Rohtang Pass on foot, a young girl against a snowbound pass, until the path eventually led to a medical college in Dehradun.

Yet nothing in this story is the story of a seeker. Mataji herself says she has never practiced Sadhana of any kind: no Gyaan, no Yog, no Dhyaan, nothing of the sort. Nor was she ever drawn to them. What she carries was never built through practice. She has always been what she is.

Not a Guru of the jungle, but a light for the world.

The Second Door

The Divine

Everyone saw it. She refused to claim it.

Some are known by what they claim. She is known by what others cannot help but see.

From her earliest childhood and in later years, Saints, Sadhus, Monks and Pirs across faiths looked at her and recognised divinity within. She turned it away. It was in Rishikesh, on the banks of the Ganga, that the river held up a mirror and she saw her true reflection.

While she is Dr. Monika Parsheera to her patients, to her followers and devotees she is beloved Mataji. To the Sadhus and Saints who recognise her, she is Maa Someshwari Devi, a name spoken with worship, for they see in her the incarnation of Ma Durga, the Hindu Goddess. Her disciples and followers speak of their experience in her presence: precognition, faith healing and miracles, and the same sense of worship.

She is the epitome of divine grace. And so they come from far away, touched by her love, compassion, grace and energy, simply to sit with her and meet her silence. When seekers began to settle near her home, hoping to become her disciples, the Himalaya Niketan Ashram was born.

Light

A feeling, indescribable, growing, without any effort of oneself.

Compassion

More than forgiving, Mataji understands. You can show your true face without fear.

Silence

Sitting quietly with Mataji is a conversation in itself. Presence alone transforms.

The Third Door

The Eternal

Liberation is for here and now.

Mataji points to a living state of awareness and equanimity: the drop merging with the infinite while still in the body. Liberation is not something to be attained after death. It is experienced in the body, here and now.

She walks a path for the 21st century. She lives within society, never above it, and guides seekers to move through life with awareness, to enjoy it, and to see it for what it is. Hers is a practical road, never extreme. Her mission is simple: to help others meet their true self.

Her presence is light itself, and it is transformative. In it, guards drop, masks fall, and the seeker is lifted to a higher level, to higher vibrations, without any effort, like boats lifted by a rising tide. There is no ladder of techniques to climb; whatever happens, happens only through Guru Kripa, the Guru’s grace.

“Even with a moment of Truth within you, Anything is possible.”Mataji
“Sabke liye sab nahi hai.”
Every path unfolds in its own time.Mataji
